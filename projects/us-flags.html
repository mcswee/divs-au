American state flags are, for the most part, pretty terrible. Too many are seals on bedsheets: intricate, overly complicated emblems designed for official documents, repurposed as a state's primary symbol of identity and slapped onto a plain blue background. The seal is too detailed to work well as a flag, and the background does nothing to make it distinctive. Others ditch the seal but substitute generic American imagery: eagles, stars, stripes and the obligatory red, white and blue. It's all very rah-rah America, but says remarkably little about what makes the state itself unique.

Even states with good design elements often undermine them with generic colours, unnecessary seals or text, awkward proportions or symbolism they'd be better off leaving behind. Confederate imagery remains a particular problem in the South, whether quietly retained or, like in Georgia's case, barely disguised at all. But the North isn't immune to questionable historical symbolism either, as Massachusetts demonstrates. There are only a handful of state flags that get most of this right. The rest range from bland and forgettable to outright failures.

Design principles

These redesigns follow my own set of principles, focused on fixing the problems above while retaining each state's character and making the collection feel like a coherent whole. There are influential guides to flag design that are often treated as hard-and-fast rules. I don't subscribe to that approach. My aim is to create flags that work visually, represent their states meaningfully and avoid genetic pitfalls.

  • All redesigns are standardised to a 2:3 ratio. The days of 22:37 proportions are over.
  • State names have been removed because a flag is not a name badge. Other text can remain or be added where it serves a meaningful purpose.
  • Overt Confederate imagery has been significantly altered. Where symbols or imagery still reflect Confederate symbols, I aim to change at least two elements to distinguish the modern state from its Confederate past. Same for other problematic symbols such as appropriating Native American imagery.
  • Generic American tropes and default red, white and blue palettes are replaced with relevant local symbols and colours that have a genuine connection to the state.
