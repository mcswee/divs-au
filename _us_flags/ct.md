critique: |
  Connecticut's current flag is a classic seal-on-a-bedsheet design. The three grapevines on the seal are also a poor choice of symbolism, having no meaningful historical or cultural connection to Connecticut.
description: |
  My design for Connecticut is a vertical bicolor of white and purple. The purple fly-side stripe features an eradicated oak tree, while three purple stars are placed on the white hoist-side field.
symbolism:
  - title: "The Three Colonies"
    text: |
      The three stars represent the three original Connecticut colonies of Hartford, Windsor, and Wethersfield.
  - title: "The State Colors"
    text: |
      Purple is retained from the state coat of arms, where it is used for the three grapevines. While the grapevines themselves are replaced, their color association is retained.
  - title: "The Charter Oak"
    text: |
      The oak tree represents the Charter Oak, an iconic symbol of Connecticut's resistance to British authority. Its eradicated roots continue the transplanted theme established by the grapevines.
